## PART TWO · THE TERMINUS

### 1.1 Four Hebrew roots stand at the rest-position, and none names recovery from fatigue

**Šābat**, the hand coming off the work. Genesis 2:2, *va-yekhal Elohim ba-yom ha-shevi'i melakhto asher asah, **va-yishbot** ba-yom ha-shevi'i mi-kol melakhto*.

**Nûaḥ**, the alighting that stays. Genesis 8:4, *va-**tanaḥ** ha-tevah*, and the ark came to rest.

**Shaqat**, to be undisturbed, used as a terminal-state refrain. Judges 3:11, 3:30, 5:31, 8:28, *va-**tishqot** ha-aretz arbaʿim shanah*, and the land was quiet forty years. **A formula the corpus repeats four times to mark the achieved state after the deliverer's work, and never to mark the work.**

**Rāgaʿ**, relief found on arrival. **Jeremiah 6:16**, *imdu al derakhim u-re'u ve-sha'alu **li-netivot olam**, ei zeh derekh ha-tov u-lekhu vah, u-mitz'u **margoʿa** le-nafshotekhem*, stand at the roads and look, and ask for **the ancient paths**, where the good way is, and walk in it, and find **rest for your souls**.

**That verse is the seed in one sentence and the corpus wrote it.** The paths are *olam*, already there and not to be built. The instruction is to ask and to walk, not to await. The terminus is *margoʿa*, a state, and it is *le-nafshotekhem*, for your own souls, so it is entered and not delivered. **And the verse closes on the refusal: *va-yomeru lo nelekh*, but they said, we will not walk in it.** Deposit, road, terminus, and non-entry, in one verse, with the non-entry stated as a choice of the hearers.

**The four are not synonyms and each carries a distinct office.** *Šābat* is **cessation**, the hand coming off the work, and its subject is the worker. *Nûaḥ* is **settlement**, the alighting that stays, and its subject is the mover. *Šāqaṭ* is **undisturbedness**, quiet from what would trouble from outside, and its subject is the land. *Rāgaʿ* is **relief**, repose reached at the end of a walk, and its subject is the soul. **Cessation, settlement, quiet, relief: four faces of one position, and not one of them a fatigue word.** The seventh position is a state held at no cost, not a recuperation taken.

*Replacement note recorded on the face: an earlier form of this card counted the completion root as the fourth. A completion word is not a rest word, the two exhibits offered for it were a man arriving whole and a building finished, and the member is withdrawn and replaced by rāgaʿ, which is a rest word and which carries the entry-failure in its own second clause.*

*Replacement note: v1 reached four roots by crossing into a second language. The Hebrew Bible supplies four on its own, and the argument is stronger for not leaving the corpus.*

### 1.2 The terminus is named, and it is named in the second of those roots

- **Deuteronomy 12:9.** *Ki lo batem ad attah el **ha-menuḥah** ve-el ha-naḥalah*, for you have not yet come to the rest and to the inheritance. With 12:10, *ve-**heniaḥ** lakhem mi-kol oyveikhem mi-saviv*.
- **Joshua 21:44.** *Va-**yanaḥ** YHWH lahem mi-saviv*, and the LORD gave them rest round about. Repeated at 22:4 and 23:1.
- **1 Kings 8:56**, at the Temple's dedication. *Barukh YHWH asher natan **menuḥah** le-ammo Yisrael ke-khol asher dibber*.
- **1 Chronicles 22:9**, defining the temple-builder before his birth. *Ish **menuḥah** yihyeh, va-**haniḥoti** lo mi-kol oyvav mi-saviv*.
- **Ezekiel 37:14**, the ingathering's own terminal clause. *Ve-**hinnaḥti** etkhem al admatkhem*.
- **Psalm 95:11.** *Asher nishbaʿti ve-appi im yevo'un el **menuḥati***, to whom I swore in My wrath that they should not enter My rest.
- **Isaiah 11:9.** *Ki mal'ah ha-aretz deʿah et YHWH ka-mayim la-yam mekhassim.* A state description with **no agent in the clause**.
- **Isaiah 2:4.** *Lo yissa goy el goy ḥerev ve-**lo yilmedu od milḥamah***. A cessation, which is *šābat* in narrative form.

### 1.3 Four facts, each checkable inside the corpus

**One, the terminus is named rather than inferred**, and its word is the cognate of the word at the terminal position of the creation account.

**Two, it was delivered without a messiah.** Joshua AS at 21:44. Solomon AS announcing it as already given at 1 Kings 8:56. The Judges refrain at 3:11 and 3:30 and 5:31 and 8:28 delivering *shaqat* four separate times under four ordinary deliverers. **A terminus that ordinary agents deliver, repeatedly, is not a terminus requiring an extraordinary one.**

**Three, it was recorded as not entered.** Psalm 95:11, of the same object, in the same vocabulary.

**Four, the corpus prices the exile in unentered rest.** 2 Chronicles 36:21, the land lying desolate *ad ratzetah ha-aretz et shabbetoteha*, until the land had enjoyed her sabbaths, on Leviticus 26:34-35 and 43, *az tirtzeh ha-aretz et shabbetoteha* because it did not rest in your sabbaths while you dwelt on it. **The national catastrophe is accounted in sabbaths owed and not in years served.** The rest is not skippable. It is only optional in who takes it.

**And repeated non-entry is not evidence that the object does no work, which is the sharpest objection available here.** The corpus records the rest as **entered** and not merely offered: the *shaqat* refrain reports the land quiet for forty and eighty years at a stretch at Judges 3:11, 3:30, 5:31 and 8:28, which is occupancy; and 1 Kings 8:56 announces it as already given, in the past tense, at the height of the monarchy. **The pattern is entered, lost, offered again, declined, offered again**, which is exactly the shape of a state guarded by *zakhor* and lost by *shakhaḥ* across generations. **A state nobody ever occupies would be indistinguishable from an unavailable one; the corpus reports occupancy five times.**

### 1.4 The terminus is stated repeatedly with no figure in the clause

Micah 6:8, *higgid lekha adam mah tov... ki im asot mishpat ve-ahavat ḥesed ve-hatzneʿa lekhet im elohekha*, the whole requirement in three infinitives with nothing left over. Deuteronomy 30:11-14, the word not hidden, not in heaven, not beyond the sea, *ki qarov eleykha ha-davar meod, be-fikha u-vilvavkha la-asotho*. Deuteronomy 10:12-13, five infinitives, no eschatology in any. Jeremiah 9:22-23, by subtraction, strike wisdom and might and riches and what remains is knowing Him. Hosea 6:6, *ki ḥesed ḥafatzti ve-lo zavaḥ*.

**Salvation is already here and it never left.**

## PART THREE · THE ENTRY PROBLEM

**Deuteronomy 29:3.** *Ve-lo natan YHWH lakhem **lev la-daʿat ve-einayim lir'ot ve-oznayim lishmoaʿ** ad ha-yom ha-zeh.* The deliverable was delivered; forty years of signs are recited in the preceding verses. **The receiving organ is stated as not given.**

**Isaiah 6:9-10.** *Shimʿu shamoaʿ ve-al tavinu*, and then the operative clause, *hashmen lev ha-am ha-zeh ve-oznav hakhbed ve-einav hashaʿ, pen yir'eh... va-shav ve-rafa lo.* **At the moment of commissioning the corpus states that the transmission will run faithfully into a reception that cannot process it**, and 6:11 asks *ad matai*, receiving a duration rather than a resolution.

**Isaiah 42:19-20.** *Mi ivver ki im **avdi**, ve-ḥeresh **ke-mal'akhi eshlaḥ***, closing on *paqoaḥ oznayim ve-lo yishmaʿ*. **The carrier is named blind and deaf, of the carrier**, inside the Servant Songs, seven chapters above 49:2's *va-yasimeni le-ḥetz barur, be-ashpato histirani*.

**Deuteronomy 8:12-14.** *Pen tokhal ve-savaʿta... ve-**ram levavekha ve-shakhaḥta***. **The danger condition is prosperity, not hardship**, and the failure mode is forgetting while standing in the good state. A rest entered and unnoticed is, to the one in it, indistinguishable from a rest never given.

**And the corpus binds the seventh position to remembrance in the commandment itself.** Exodus 20:8, ***zakhor*** *et yom ha-shabbat le-qaddesho*. **The one word of the ten that opens on an imperative of memory guards the one position that is a state.** Forgetting un-settles; remembering keeps. Deuteronomy 8's failure and Exodus 20's guard are the same mechanism read from its two ends.

**The strongest objection here is that two of the four state the organ as not given and one has the ears made heavy, so the deficit looks imposed rather than incurred, which would move the locus off the receiver. The corpus answers it, and the answer is a fifth text.**

**Jeremiah 6:16** states the same non-entry as a refusal in the hearers' own voice: the paths are *netivot olam*, ancient and standing; the instruction is to ask and to walk; the terminus is *margoʿa le-nafshotekhem*; and the verse closes *va-yomeru **lo nelekh***, and they said, we will not walk in it. **No organ is withheld there and nothing is made heavy. A road is offered, a rest is named, and the hearers decline in direct speech.** Deuteronomy 30:19 is the same shape from the other side, *u-**vaḥarta** ba-ḥayyim*, an imperative addressed to a chooser. **The corpus carries both an unfurnished organ and a declined offer and holds them unresolved**, which is the preserved-contradiction habit operating here as well. **What it carries nowhere is a withheld terminus.**

**Five texts, three books, one direction, with the Exodus 20:8 guard beside them in a fourth.** Not one says the terminus was absent, withheld, delayed, or reserved.

## PART FOUR · THE CONCESSION ENGINE

**The refusal.** Judges 8:22-23. *Lo emshol ani bakhem ve-lo yimshol beni bakhem, **YHWH yimshol bakhem***.

**The demand, and its content.** 1 Samuel 8:5, *simah lanu melekh le-shofteinu **ke-khol ha-goyim***. Judges already existed and the request replaces them, so **the demand is for a visible sovereign of the kind the neighbours have**, a request about form and not function.

**The diagnosis, and the clause read too fast.** 1 Samuel 8:7, *ki **oti** ma'asu **mi-melokh** aleihem*. It does not say the reign ended, was suspended, or transferred. It says the reign was rejected **as theirs to be under**. **The sovereignty is unaffected; its acceptance, and therefore its visibility, is what changed.**

**The prosecution, itemized in advance.** 1 Samuel 8:11-18: sons, daughters, fields, vineyards, olive groves, a tenth of the seed, a tenth of the flock, the best young men, the donkeys, *ve-attem tihyu lo la-avadim*. ## PART FIVE · THE DISGUISE

**The throne is God's throne with a man on it, and it is not one clause.** 1 Chronicles 29:23, *va-yeshev Shelomoh al **kisse YHWH** le-melekh*. 1 Chronicles 28:5, the chosen son *la-shevet al **kisse malkhut YHWH** al Yisrael*. And 2 Chronicles 9:8, *le-titkha al kis'o le-melekh **la-YHWH elohekha***, to set you on **His** throne as king **for the LORD your God**. **Three occurrences, one book, one construction, and in the third the beneficiary of the kingship is named as God and not the king.** And the same book states the ownership outright at 1 Chronicles 29:11, *lekha YHWH ha-mamlakhah*, thine, O LORD, is the kingdom, **spoken by David AS twelve verses before Solomon AS is seated.** An honorific does not survive being paired with an explicit assertion of ownership by the honoree's own predecessor. The seat is not the king's.

**And the conferral grammar recurs.** Psalm 110:1, *ne'um YHWH **la-adoni** shev li-mini*, the LORD says to **my lord**, a human superior, sit at My right hand, where the seat is offered and the offerer is named.

**And the same psalm confers the priesthood from outside Israel.** Psalm 110:4, *attah khohen le-olam **al divrati Malki-Tzedeq***, you are a priest forever **after the order of Melchizedek**, and not after the order of Aaron. Genesis 14:18 gives that order its only occupant: *u-Malki-Tzedeq **melekh Shalem**... ve-hu **khohen le-El Elyon***, a king-priest of the city that becomes Jerusalem, **pre-Israelite**, to whom Abraham AS gives a tenth, and whose divine title Abram AS then takes onto his own oath at 14:22, *YHWH **El Elyon** qoneh shamayim va-aretz*. **One psalm supplies both the conferral of the throne and a priestly order patterned on a Canaanite king-priest**, and neither is the king's own. Daniel 7:14, *ve-leh **yehiv** sholtan*, and to him dominion **was given**, passive, with the Giver in the sentence.

**Therefore: a sovereignty wearing an occupant.** Combine 8:7 with 29:23 and the reign never transferred; only its face changed. **The visible thing is not the operative thing, and it exists because the people could not hold the operative one.** The disguise is for the viewer. They asked for a form they could see and received one, seated on a throne that was never theirs, under a statute that binds the occupant and prints his failure conditions in advance.

**The fence, and it is the corpus's.** Deuteronomy 17:20's purpose clause, *le-vilti **rum levavo** me-eḥav*, and a guard against an impossibility is not a guard. 2 Chronicles 26:16, Uzziah, *u-khe-ḥezqato **gavah libbo** ad le-hashḥit*, struck at the altar. 2 Chronicles 32:25-26, Hezekiah, *ki **gavah libbo***, then *va-yikkanaʿ*, and the office stood. Ezekiel 28:2 and 28:9, *moshav elohim yashavti* answered by *ve-attah adam ve-lo el*, **addressed to the prince of Tyre and therefore corroboration on the claim-type only**, an erratum carried from an earlier misuse in this arc. 2 Samuel 7:14's own limit inside the sonship clause, *ve-hokhaḥtiv **be-shevet anashim***, with 7:15 barring the withdrawal. Psalm 2:7 dating it in one adverb, *ani **ha-yom** yelidtikha*, a coronation-day formula, and **a sonship that has a day is a sonship conferred on it**.

**And the corpus de-escalates in its own voice.** Ezekiel 34:24, *va-**ani YHWH** ehyeh lahem **le-Elohim**, ve-**avdi David nasi be-tokham***, I the LORD will be their God, and My servant David a prince among them. **God and prince named in one verse, with the roles distinguished by the verse itself.** Isaiah 42:1, *hen **avdi***. Isaiah 52:13, *hinneh yaskil **avdi***.

***It was given* is not *I am*, and that distinction is the whole of it.**

**And the corpus supplies the hardest pressure on that distinction itself, which is stated here rather than left for a reader to find.** Exodus 23:21, of the *mal'akh* sent before the people: *hishamer mi-panav u-shmaʿ be-qolo, **al tammer bo**, ki lo yissa le-fishʿakhem, **ki shemi be-qirbo***, do not rebel against him, for he will not pardon your transgression, **for My name is within him**. That is the closest the corpus comes to placing the Name inside a sent agent, and it is canonical. **It is recorded as a tension and not resolved here**, because the same corpus that says *shemi be-qirbo* also enforces the anti-fusion sanctions at 1 Samuel 13 and 2 Chronicles 26, refuses the name under direct questioning at Genesis 32:30 and Judges 13:18, and answers *ve-attah adam ve-lo el*. **The corpus states both and reconciles neither, which is the habit at Part Six operating on the attribution question itself**, and the card takes no side that the text does not take.

**Rung two, concession.** 1 Samuel 8. A king, against an itemized bill, granted after the refusal to hear.

**Rung three, the concession made permanent.** 2 Samuel 7:12-16. The house established, the throne forever, the withdrawal barred at 7:15. **The thing conceded under protest is now unrevokable.**

**Rung four, cosmic vocabulary.** Psalm 2:7's *ani ha-yom yelidtikha*. Psalm 45:7's *kis'akha **Elohim** olam va-ed*, addressed to the king. Psalm 110:1's seat at the right hand. **The occupant acquires the vocabulary of the seat.**

**Rung five, the throne-name.** Isaiah 9:5, *pele yoʿetz **el gibbor** avi ad sar shalom*. Four titles on a child, one of them divine.

**Rung six, regrowth after the felling.** Isaiah 11:1, *choter mi-**geza** Yishai*, a shoot from a felled **stump**. **The promise is re-granted over the corpse of the first grant.**

**Rung eight, celestial conferral.** Daniel 7:13-14. The *bar enash* on the clouds, dominion *yehiv*, given, everlasting.

**Eight rungs, six books. The ordering claim is scoped, and the scoping is stated against this card's own interest.** Rungs one, two, and three stand in **narrative sequence within the corpus itself**, Judges to Samuel, refusal to concession to permanence, and that ordering is the corpus's and requires no dating. ## PART SEVEN · THE LIFE IS NOT IN THE WOOD

**The corpus splits the felled tree into three parts and says which one is dead.**

**Job 14:7-9.** *Ki yesh la-**etz** **tiqvah**, im yikkaret od yaḥalif, ve-yonaqto lo teḥdal · im yazqin ba-aretz **shorsho**, u-ve-afar **yamut giz'o** · me-**reaḥ mayim** yafriaḥ, ve-asah qatzir kemo nata.* **There is hope for a tree**, that if cut down it will sprout again and its shoot will not cease; though its **root** grow old in the earth and **its stump die in the dust**, at the **scent of water** it buds and makes branches like a new plant.

**Three parts, three verdicts, stated in three clauses.** The *etz*, the tree, **has hope**. The *geza*, the stump, **dies in the dust**. The *shoresh*, the root, **persists in the earth**. And the trigger is *me-reaḥ mayim*, **the scent of water**, which is water already present in the ground and detected, not water delivered.

**Isaiah 11:1 names both parts and the corpus has already told us which is dead.** *Choter mi-**geza** Yishai, ve-**netzer mi-shorashav** yifreh*, a shoot from the **stump** of Jesse, and a branch from **his roots** shall grow. Those are not one image said twice; **they name two sources, and Job 14:8 has already killed one of them.**

**A seventh preserved pair, stated on both ends and reconciled by neither.** Isaiah 11:1 draws the shoot **from the stump**; Job 14:8 says **the stump dies**. The corpus states both and resolves neither, which is the habit at Part Six operating on the very image the messianic figure is named from.

**And the corpus supplies the error by name, which is the finding of this part.** The failure is not expecting a shoot. **The failure is addressing the wood as the source.**

**Jeremiah 2:27**, *omerim **la-etz avi attah**, ve-la-even at yelidtani*, saying **to the wood, you are my father**, and to the stone, you gave me birth.

**Habakkuk 2:19-20**, and it is the exact shape of the messianic demand. *Hoy omer **la-etz haqitzah**, **uri** le-even dumam · **hu yoreh?** hinneh hu tafus zahav va-khesef, **ve-khol ruaḥ ein be-qirbo**.* **Woe to him who says to the wood, Awake, and to the dumb stone, Arise. Shall it teach? It is overlaid with gold and silver, and there is no breath in it at all.** And then 2:20, *va-YHWH **be-heikhal qodsho**, has mi-panav kol ha-aretz*, **but the LORD is in His holy temple; let all the earth be silent before Him.**

**Two verses, and the contrast is the whole card.** On one side, a thing addressed with *arise*, which cannot teach and has no breath in it. On the other, **One already present, requiring silence rather than summoning.** The error is not hope. **The error is telling the wood to get up.**

**And the corpus attaches the perception-failure to the wood-error in the same passage.** Isaiah 44 runs the extended prosecution: a man plants, fells, burns half for bread, and of the remainder makes a thing to which he says *hatzileni **ki eli attah***, deliver me, for you are my god. And the diagnosis at 44:18, *lo yadeʿu ve-lo yavinu, **ki taḥ me-re'ot eineihem, me-haskil libbotam***, they do not know and do not understand, for **their eyes are besmeared from seeing and their hearts from understanding**, with 44:19's *ha-lo **sheqer bi-mini***, is there not a lie in my right hand.

**That is Isaiah 6:10's mechanism, in the same book, attached to the wood.** Eyes that do not see and a heart that does not understand, named at the exact point where a man takes the cut timber for the source. **The deaf-carrier diagnosis and the wood-error are one diagnosis in one prophet**, which means the entry-failure of Part Two and the misplacement of the life are the same fault seen from two sides.

**And Isaiah 6 itself ends on the stump.** After *shimʿu shamoaʿ ve-al tavinu*, after *ad matai*, after the desolation, 6:13 closes: *ka-elah ve-kha-allon **asher be-shallekhet matzevet bam**, **zeraʿ qodesh matzavtah***, as a terebinth and an oak whose **stump** remains when they are felled, **the holy seed is its stump**. **The commission into deafness terminates on a felled tree that still holds the seed**, seven chapters before the shoot at 11:1. Daniel 4 carries the same object once more, *iqqar sharshohi be-arʿa shevuqu*, leave the stump of its roots in the earth.

**The finding.** The visible shoot is real and the tree's hope is real, *yesh la-etz tiqvah*. **But the life is not in the stump, the corpus says so in the dust, and what persists is the root and what triggers is water already in the ground.** The figure is the emergence and not the source, which is the same sort the map runs at Part Eight: **the shoot is act-indexed and the life that pushes it is not.** Address the emergence as the source and you have said *arise* to the wood, and Habakkuk has already answered that there is no breath in it and that the One who has breath is present and asks for silence.

## PART FIFTEEN · THE TRANSMISSION, THE CUSTODY, AND THE NINE PAIRS

**In v1 the displacement mechanism was carried by a second corpus. The Hebrew Bible names it itself, and more sharply.**

**Jeremiah 8:8.** *Eikhah tomeru ḥakhamim anaḥnu ve-torat YHWH ittanu, aken hinneh **la-sheqer asah et sheqer soferim***. How do you say, we are wise and the teaching of the LORD is with us, when behold, **the lying pen of the scribes has made it into a lie**.

**That is the corpus indicting the scribal pen, in the corpus, by name.** And note what it does not say. It does not say the teaching was destroyed. It says the pen made it into a lie **while the teaching is still with them**, *ve-torat YHWH ittanu*, in the same sentence. **Deposit present, carriage corrupted, text in hand.** Displacement and not deletion, stated in-corpus, and the *ittanu* is the load-bearing word.

**The contested term is scoped, and the finding holds on either construal.** *Soferim* is disputed between manuscript copyists and a scholarly class whose instruction is at issue, and the parsing of *asah* is likewise disputed. **Both readings put the fault at the carriage and neither puts it at the deposit**, because *ve-torat YHWH ittanu* stands in the same sentence under either. Copyists corrupting a text still in hand, or teachers corrupting an instruction still in hand, are the same structural claim, and the card takes no side between them.

**And the corpus does not rest the finding on one verse.** Ezekiel 22:26 has the priests doing violence to the teaching, *kohaneha ḥamesu torati*, and hiding their eyes from the sabbaths, *u-mi-shabbetotai heʿlimu eineihem*, **which puts the sabbath and the corrupted carriage in a single verse.** Malachi 2:7-8 states the office and its breach in two clauses, *ki siftei khohen yishmeru daʿat ve-torah yevaqshu mi-pihu*, for the priest's lips should keep knowledge, then *attem sartem min ha-derekh, hikhshaltem rabbim ba-torah*, you have turned from the way and made many stumble in the teaching. **Three sites, three books, one direction: the office of carriage failing while the deposit remains nameable.**

**And the corpus anticipates tampering in its own statute.** Deuteronomy 4:2 and 13:1, *lo tosifu al ha-davar... ve-lo tigreu mimmennu*, do not add and do not subtract. **A prohibition on adding and subtracting is issued only where adding and subtracting are expected.**

**Which is why the preservation is the decisive datum.** Judges 8:23 sits in the canon. 1 Samuel 8:7 sits in the canon. The itemized bill and the unanswered cry sit in the canon. Hosea 13:11 sits in the canon. Copied and taught in the same scroll as Psalm 45:7 and Isaiah 9:5. **A transmission running by deletion does not preserve its own prosecution.**

**Ten canonical pairs, each stated on both ends, resolved by neither.** The tenth is the pastoral pair seated at Part Twenty and is listed here with the rest so the count and the table agree.

**And the corpus withholds origin at three source-points, which is a pattern and not three silences.**

**Melchizedek** appears at Genesis 14:18 with no father, no mother, no genealogy, no birth and no death, occupies three verses, and supplies the order the Davidic priesthood is patterned on at Psalm 110:4. **Tamar** appears at Genesis 38:6 as *ishah... u-shemah Tamar*, a wife whose name is given and whose people, father, and origin are not, and she is the mother of Perez and therefore the origin point of the line at Ruth 4:18. **Elijah AS** appears at 1 Kings 17:1 as *Eliyahu ha-Tishbi mi-toshavei Gilʿad*, with no father named in a corpus that names fathers, and he does not die at 2 Kings 2:11.

**Three figures at three load-bearing junctions, the priestly order, the royal origin, and the forerunner, and in each the corpus supplies the emergence and withholds the source.** That is the stump-and-root finding at the genealogical register: **what pushes is not given, and what appears is.**

**And the line is seated through the bar, systematically, in every named generation.**

Deuteronomy 7:1-3 lists the seven nations and commands *ve-**lo titḥatten bam***, you shall not intermarry with them, with Exodus 34:15-16 beside it. Against that standing bar the corpus runs the ancestry:

**Ishmael AS**, an Egyptian mother at Genesis 16:1, *Hagar ha-**Mitzrit***, and an Egyptian wife at 21:21, taken *me-eretz Mitzrayim*. **Joseph AS**, married at Genesis 41:45 to Asenath *bat Poti-Feraʿ **khohen On***, the daughter of an Egyptian priest, from whom come Ephraim and Manasseh, whom Jacob AS then claims as his own at 48:5. **Judah**, a Canaanite wife at Genesis 38:2, *bat ish **Kenaʿani** u-shemo Shuaʿ*, **whose three sons are not the line**, which routes instead through Tamar. **Moses AS**, Zipporah the Midianite at Exodus 2:21, and *ha-ishah ha-**Kushit*** at Numbers 12:1, **where Miriam and Aaron object and the corpus prosecutes the objectors and not the marriage.** **Boaz**, Ruth *ha-**Moaviyyah***, named so four times over, to Obed to Jesse to David AS. **David AS**, Bathsheba of the household of *Uriyyah **ha-Ḥitti*** at 2 Samuel 11:3. And **Solomon AS**, anointed at 1 Kings 1:39, *va-yimshaḥ oto*, whose foreign wives are listed at 11:1 as Moabite, Ammonite, Edomite, Sidonian and Hittite, **with the bar quoted verbatim at 11:2 at the moment of the breach**, *min ha-goyim asher amar YHWH... lo tavo'u vahem*, and whose successor at 14:21 is the son of *Naʿamah **ha-Ammonit***.

**Seven instances, and the heading is corrected here because the list is not seven generations of one line.** The Davidic ancestry proper is enumerable and the corpus enumerates it: Ruth 4:18-22 runs Perez to Hezron to Ram to Amminadab to Nahshon to Salmon to Boaz to Obed to Jesse to David, **ten names**, and of the seven instances above **four stand inside that line**, Judah at its head, Boaz through Ruth *ha-Moaviyyah*, David AS through the household of *Uriyyah ha-Ḥitti*, and Solomon AS whose successor is the son of *Naʿamah ha-Ammonit*. **Three stand outside it**, Ishmael AS in the Abrahamic branch that does not run to David, Joseph AS in the Ephraimite line, and Moses AS in Levi. **The corrected finding is stronger for the split**: the pattern is not confined to the royal ancestry, it runs across the patriarchal, prophetic, and royal registers alike, and it is therefore **a feature of how the corpus seats offices in general** rather than a peculiarity of one genealogy. **One standing bar, seven instances across three registers, four of them in the line itself, and the corpus quotes its own bar at the point of the breach.** So it is not unaware and it is not approving. **It bars, it records the breach as a breach, and it continues the line through the breach anyway.**

**And that is the concession structure at the genealogical register.** Only God is king, and the king is granted against a printed bill. No intermarriage with the seven nations, and the line runs through them in every generation the corpus names. **The same corpus states the prohibition and seats the outcome through it, and preserves both, which is the habit at full scale rather than a single pair.** Ezekiel 16:3 then states the parentage of the city itself, *avikh ha-Emori ve-immekh Ḥittit*, repeated at 16:45.


**And the custody itself is received from outside, at four points, with the source named in every one.**

**The priestly order.** Genesis 14:18-20. Melchizedek, *khohen le-**El Elyon***, **blesses Abram AS**, and *va-yitten lo **maʿaser** mi-kol*, and he gave him a tenth. **The direction is stated in the corpus for the blessing and is contested for the tithe, and the finding survives either reading.** *Va-yevarekhehu* at 14:19 is unambiguous: **the Canaanite king-priest blesses Abram AS**. The following clause, *va-**yitten** lo maʿaser mi-kol*, and he gave him a tenth of all, **supplies no explicit subject**, and the reading is disputed between Abram AS giving to Melchizedek and Melchizedek giving to Abram AS. **This card takes no side.** On the first reading the patriarch pays and the custody direction is explicit. On the second the blessing still runs from the outsider inward and the tenth is a provision rather than a payment. **In neither does the order become Israel's own**, because Psalm 110:4 settles that independently of the antecedent: the priesthood is *al divrati Malki-Tzedeq* and **not after Aaron**, which is a statement about whose order it is and not about who handed whom a tenth. Psalm 110:4 then makes it permanent and not incidental, *khohen **le-olam** al divrati Malki-Tzedeq*.

**And it is a local title-form and not a single figure, which the corpus supplies without reconstruction.** Joshua 10:1 names the king of Jerusalem at the conquest as ***Adoni-Tzedeq***, my lord is *tzedeq*, against Genesis 14:18's ***Malki-Tzedeq***, my king is *tzedeq*. **One city, one theophoric element, two rulers, both in the Hebrew Bible.** So the order the Davidic priesthood is patterned on at Psalm 110:4 is not an isolated encounter but **a standing Jerusalemite form the corpus records twice**, and the second occurrence stands inside the conquest narrative where the city is taken.

**The divine title.** Genesis 14:19-20 puts *El Elyon **qoneh shamayim va-aretz*** in Melchizedek's mouth, and 14:22 has Abram AS take that exact formula onto his own oath with the Name attached: *harimoti yadi el **YHWH El Elyon qoneh shamayim va-aretz***. **The title is the Canaanite priest's and the patriarch appends YHWH to it, in the same chapter, four verses later.**

**The judicial structure.** Exodus 18. Jethro, *khohen Midyan*, offers *olah u-zevaḥim le-Elohim* at 18:12, and at 18:13-23 supplies the entire delegated judiciary, rulers of thousands, hundreds, fifties and tens. And 18:24 records the transfer without softening it: *va-**yishmaʿ Mosheh le-qol ḥotno** va-yaʿas kol asher amar*, and Moses AS **listened to the voice of his father-in-law and did all that he said.**

**The Temple ground.** The Jebusites hold Jerusalem at 2 Samuel 5:6 until David AS takes it, and the sanctuary stands on ground **bought** from one of them: 2 Samuel 24:24, *lo ki **qano eqneh** me-otekha **bi-meḥir**, ve-lo aʿaleh la-YHWH Elohai olot **ḥinnam***, no, I will surely buy it from you at a price, and I will not offer to the LORD my God burnt offerings that cost me nothing; with the weight recorded at 1 Chronicles 21:25 and the site identified at 2 Chronicles 3:1, *be-**goren Ornan ha-Yevusi***, on the threshing floor of Ornan **the Jebusite**.

**And a fifth item sits beside the four, where a naming is left uncorrected.** Genesis 16:7-13. The *mal'akh YHWH* finds **Hagar**, the Egyptian, at a spring in the wilderness, promises her descendants beyond number, which is a divine prerogative, and she names the one who spoke to her: *va-tiqra shem YHWH ha-dover eleha **attah El Ro'i***, you are a God who sees me, with 16:13's *ha-gam **halom ra'iti aḥarei ro'i***. **The text does not correct her.** No rebuke, no clarification, no demotion follows. **A naming performed by an Egyptian slavewoman stands in the corpus uncorrected**, and the son promised there is Ishmael AS, whose line the seating-through-the-bar pattern above already runs.

**And the corpus withholds the name in return, twice, in the same construction.** Genesis 32:30, Jacob AS asks and is refused: *lammah zeh **tish'al li-shmi***. Judges 13:18, the same refusal with a reason: *lammah zeh tish'al li-shmi **ve-hu feli***, seeing it is **wondrous**. **She names and is not corrected; they ask and are not answered.** That belongs with Melchizedek, Tamar, and Elijah AS in the withheld-origin cluster: **the corpus supplies the encounter and withholds the source, and here it withholds it under direct questioning.**

**Four custody items, and the genus is stated because they are not four of a kind.** What is common to them is not their category but their **transfer**: in each, something Israel holds is recorded as having come from outside, with the outside source named. **A standing order** at Genesis 14:18 with Psalm 110:4. **A divine title** at Genesis 14:19 taken onto the patriarch's oath at 14:22. **An administrative procedure** at Exodus 18:13-24. **A parcel of ground** at 2 Chronicles 3:1 with the price at 2 Samuel 24:24. Order, title, procedure, ground: **four categories, one transfer-relation, and the relation is what the finding is about.** Four named foreign sources, and not one of them concealed. Priesthood, divine title, judiciary, and the ground the house stands on. **The corpus records the transfer, records the direction, and in the fourth case records the price and refuses to take it free.**

**Which settles what kind of preservation this is.** The consort pattern above is the same declaration at the genealogical register: Ruth is *ha-Moaviyyah* four times over, Naamah is *ha-Ammonit* twice, Uriah is *ha-Ḥitti*, Asenath's father is *khohen On*, Hagar is *ha-Mitzrit*, and 1 Kings 11:2 quotes the prohibition at the point of the breach. **A transmission concealing contraband does not label it by nation at every occurrence.** The outside element is not hidden in the text; **it is declared in the text and lost in the reading**, which is precisely where Deuteronomy 29:3, Isaiah 6:9-10, and Isaiah 44:18 place every other failure in this card.

**And the late-composition premise, if granted, cuts toward this finding rather than against it.** A hand with centuries of room and a motive to smooth would **remove** the Jebusite from the Temple site, drop the tithe paid to a Canaanite priest, and quietly lose the Midianite's judiciary. **Those are the easy deletions and none of them was made.** The threshing floor kept its owner's name and his nation; the tenth stayed paid; Moses AS stayed listening. **Whatever room existed was not used to delete, which is the same result Part Six reaches from the prosecution texts and reaches here from the custody texts.**

**One further pass of the strip, and it survives.** Remove every eschatological figure and the custody transfers still stand: the sanctuary is still on purchased ground with the seller named, the priestly order is still patterned on Salem and not on Aaron, the judiciary is still Jethro's, and the title still carries the appended Name. **Institution-indexed, not figure-indexed, and therefore surviving.**

**And indirect disclosure is the only available form, and the corpus specifies it.** Isaiah 6:9-10 is a commission to speak into a reception that cannot process the message. A message that must be transmitted and cannot be received directly has one available shape: **delivered in the form the receiver will accept, with the correcting material preserved intact alongside it.** That is what the corpus does. **The King is what the receiver accepted. The prosecution and the terminus are what was preserved beside it.**

## PART SIXTEEN · THE SELF-INSTRUMENTED CORPUS

At every point where an external instrument would be needed, **the Hebrew Bible supplies its own, and the single-corpus constraint makes each one canonical where v1 had it post-canonical.**

**Its own removability test.** Micah 6:8 and Deuteronomy 30:11-14 execute the strip in the corpus's own voice.

**Its own falsification criterion, and this replaces a twelfth-century one.** Deuteronomy 18:21-22: *ve-khi tomar bilvavekha **eikhah nedaʿ** et ha-davar asher lo dibbero YHWH · asher yedabber ha-navi be-shem YHWH **ve-lo yihyeh ha-davar ve-lo yavo**, hu ha-davar asher lo dibbero YHWH.* **A stated question, a stated criterion, and non-occurrence as the disconfirming condition.** That is an executable falsification test written into the Torah, and it is stronger than the Maimonidean two-stage test v1 relied on because it is canonical and because it requires nothing of the claimant's own testimony.

**Its own disqualification of the sign.** Deuteronomy 13:2-6, the wonder-worker refused **however impressive the sign**, so signs are explicitly non-probative against deviation.

**Its own prosecution of the seat.** Deuteronomy 17:20, executed at 2 Chronicles 26:16 and 32:25.

**Its own alternative to the fusion it forbids.** Zechariah 4:14 and 6:13, against the sanctions at 1 Samuel 13:8-14 and 2 Chronicles 26:16.

**Its own diagnosis of its readers.** Deuteronomy 29:3, Isaiah 6:9-10, Isaiah 42:19-20, the last placing the servant himself in the category, which forecloses the polemical use.

**Its own indictment of its scribes.** Jeremiah 8:8, with the anti-tampering statute at Deuteronomy 4:2 and 13:1.

**And its own preserved contradictions**, six pairs, unresolved.

**The term covers two operations and they are named apart.** *Instrumented* means the corpus supplies **tests**, which take a claim as their object and return a verdict on it. *Self-diagnosing* means the corpus supplies **readings of its own reception**, which take the hearer as their object and return a condition. The first group is Micah 6:8, Deuteronomy 30:11-14, Deuteronomy 18:21-22, Deuteronomy 13:2-6, Deuteronomy 17:20, Zechariah 4:14, Deuteronomy 4:2 and 13:1. The second is Deuteronomy 29:3, Isaiah 6:9-10, Isaiah 42:19-20, Jeremiah 8:8, and Jeremiah 6:16's *lo nelekh*. **A corpus that carries both is doing two things and the finding needs both**, since a test with no account of why it is not applied explains nothing about the non-entry.

**A corpus that carries its own tests yields findings that are readings and not impositions.** That is what answers the closed-authorship charge without appeal to any outside instrument.

## PART EIGHTEEN · FALSIFIERS

Three, each executable inside the Hebrew Bible alone.

**F1 · The terminus falsifier.** If the *menuḥah*, *shaqat*, knowledge-condition and war-cessation clauses can be shown to be figure-indexed, requiring a messianic agent in the clause or its pericope, the seed's first clause fails. **Executable**: run the *nûaḥ*, *menuḥah*, and *shaqat* concordance over the whole corpus and count agent-requirements. **Null**: the sites carry none, and four of them name ordinary deliverers.

**F2 · The concession falsifier.** If the corpus can be shown at any point to **withdraw** the concession rather than re-grant it at higher intensity, the engine and the ladder both fail. **Executable**: search for any withdrawal formula. **Null**: none exists; 2 Samuel 7:15 explicitly bars withdrawal and Hosea 13:11's taking is a judgment on the king and not a repeal.

**F3 · The receiver falsifier.** If Deuteronomy 29:3, Psalm 95:11, Isaiah 6:9-10, and Isaiah 42:19-20 can be read as locating the failure in an **absent or withheld terminus** rather than a deficient receiver, the entry problem dissolves. **Executable**: identify the subject of the deficiency in each. **Null**: all four place it in the heart, the ears, the eyes, or the hearing.

## PART TWENTY · THE UNDER-SHEPHERD, THE GAP, AND THE ACTUALIZING HABIT

### 20.1 The king is a sheep, and the corpus says so in the king's own voice

**Psalm 23:1**, ascribed to David AS, the reigning king: *YHWH **roʿi** lo eḥsar*, the LORD is **my shepherd**, I shall not want. **The man who is shepherd over Israel says he is a sheep.**

**And the commission itself is written in that grammar.** 2 Samuel 5:2, at the anointing: *attah **tirʿeh et ammi** et Yisrael, ve-attah tihyeh **le-nagid** al Yisrael*, **you shall shepherd My people Israel, and you shall be prince over Israel.** Two clauses, and the possessive in the first is decisive: ***ammi***, **My** people. **The flock is never the shepherd's.** Psalm 78:70-71 states the same at the appointment, taking him from the sheepfolds *lirʿot be-Yaʿaqov **ammo***, to shepherd Jacob **His** people.

**And the flock-relation and the hearing condition stand in one verse, in the psalm that already carries the terminus.** Psalm 95:7: *ki hu Eloheinu, va-anaḥnu **am marʿito ve-tzon yado**; **ha-yom im be-qolo tishmaʿu***, for He is our God and we are the people of His pasture and the sheep of His hand; **today, if you will hear His voice.** Then 95:11, *im yevo'un el **menuḥati***. **Sheep of His hand, then the hearing, then the unentered rest, in that order, in one psalm.** The shepherd-relation is not a separate image from the terminus; it is the terminus text's own way of naming who is being addressed.

### 20.2 Ezekiel 34 states the three tiers in one chapter, and keeps the flock God's throughout

**34:2-10**, the shepherds of Israel are indicted and removed, *hoy **roʿei Yisrael** asher hayu roʿim otam*, and the flock is taken out of their hand. **34:11-16**, God shepherds directly, in the first person and without an agent: ***ani** darashti et tzoni*, and *ani **erʿeh tzoni** va-ani arbitzem*, I will feed My flock and I will make them lie down. **34:23**, only then, *va-haqimoti aleihem **roʿeh eḥad** ve-raʿah ethen, et **avdi David***, I will set up one shepherd over them, My **servant** David. And **34:24**, the two roles named in one verse and distinguished by the verse itself: *va-**ani YHWH ehyeh lahem le-Elohim**, ve-**avdi David nasi be-tokham***.

**Three tiers, stated in order, in one chapter: the Shepherd, the under-shepherd, the flock.** And the possessive never moves. It is ***tzoni*** in God's mouth throughout, **My** flock, before the under-shepherd is appointed and after. **The under-shepherd is given a charge and is not given the sheep.**

**So the figure is king over the flock and sheep under the Shepherd, at once**, and the corpus states both and reconciles neither. **That is a tenth pair**, and it is at the pastoral register where the ninth was at the priestly and the eighth at the genealogical. Psalm 23:1 has him inside the flock; 2 Samuel 5:2 has him over it; Ezekiel 34:24 has him *nasi* while God is *Elohim*. **And the objection that these are simply two ends of one delegation, and therefore not a tension at all, is the right objection and it narrows the finding.** They **are** two ends of one delegation, and that is the point rather than a defect: the pair is filed not because the two positions contradict but because **the corpus states both of a single occupant and lets neither absorb the other**, which is the same shape as the nine pairs above and is why it is counted with them. **What it would take to break it** is a passage in which the shepherd-king is placed outside the flock, or in which the flock is called his. **Neither exists**; it is *tzoni* in God's mouth before and after the appointment. Nothing in the corpus resolves the two positions and nothing needs to, because the conferral grammar already holds them: the charge is given and the flock is not transferred.

### 20.3 The gap is stated, the lower half is conferred, and the prescribed posture is fewer words

**Three registers, and One over all three.** Exodus 20:4 names them in the image-prohibition: *asher **ba-shamayim mi-maʿal**, va-asher **ba-aretz mi-taḥat**, va-asher **ba-mayim mi-taḥat la-aretz***, in heaven above, in the earth beneath, in the water under the earth. **Three, and the prohibition is one.**

**And the above-below distinction is stated and collapsed in a single verse.** Deuteronomy 4:39: *ki YHWH hu ha-Elohim **ba-shamayim mi-maʿal ve-al ha-aretz mi-taḥat**, **ein od***, in heaven above and on the earth beneath, **there is none else.** The gap is named and the same clause forecloses a second occupant of either half. Joshua 2:11 has the identical formula in the mouth of a Canaanite woman of Jericho, which is the custody pattern of Part Fifteen appearing at the confession itself.

**And the lower half is conferred, in the same grammar as the throne.** Psalm 115:16: *ha-shamayim shamayim la-YHWH, **ve-ha-aretz natan li-vnei adam***, the heavens are the LORD's, and the earth **He has given** to the sons of man. ***Natan***, given. **The human seat on earth is conferred exactly as the seat in Jerusalem is conferred, and by the same verb-family that runs *yehiv* one corpus over.** Not surrendered, not vacated, **given**.

**And the corpus prescribes the posture at the gap, and the posture is silence.** Ecclesiastes 5:1: *ki **ha-Elohim ba-shamayim ve-attah al ha-aretz**, al ken **yihyu devareykha meʿattim***, God is in heaven and you are on the earth, therefore let your words be few. Which is Habakkuk 2:20 arriving from the other side, *va-YHWH be-heikhal qodsho, **has** mi-panav kol ha-aretz*. **Two books, one instruction: at the gap, fewer words.** The corpus does not tell the earth to summon the heavens. It tells the earth to be quiet, and at Habakkuk it does so **immediately after** answering the man who told the wood to arise.

**So the gap is real, it is named, it is not dualism, and the corpus supplies its own bar against reading it as such.** *Ein od* at Deuteronomy 4:39 is in the same verse as the above-and-beneath, and there is no second power in either half; what stands below is **given** and not autonomous, and what is required at the seam is silence and not negotiation.

**A term is scoped before Part Twenty-One reads it.** *Wood* denotes two objects in this card and the shift is marked here rather than left implicit. At Job 14 and Isaiah 11 it is the **felled tree**, a living root system with a dead crown, which is why the shoot is real and the *geza* is not its source. At Jeremiah 2:27, Isaiah 44, and Habakkuk 2:19 it is **carved timber**, a manufactured article with no root at all, which is why *ruaḥ ein be-qirbo*, there is no breath in it. **The two are not the same object and the finding needs both to be different**: the error is not expecting growth from a stump, which the corpus itself expects at Isaiah 11:1. The error is addressing as a source a thing that has none, and the second passage-set is what supplies the contrast that makes the first legible.

### 20.4 The actualizing habit, and why it does not single out the figure

**The corpus refuses to leave anything in the understanding alone.** Every abstraction is driven into a person, a plot of ground, and a lineage. The covenant becomes a man, a land, and a people. The teaching becomes tablets of stone carried in a box. The presence becomes a tent, then a house, on a threshing floor with a named owner and a stated price. The kingship becomes an occupant with a copy of the law in his hand. **This is the corpus's compositional law and it is visible everywhere, not a device applied to one object.**

**And the decisive test is that it applies to the terminus as well.** The rest is not left as a condition of the soul. It is given a **geography**: *ha-**menuḥah** ve-ha-**naḥalah*** at Deuteronomy 12:9, rest and **inheritance** in one construct, a land you have not yet come to.
