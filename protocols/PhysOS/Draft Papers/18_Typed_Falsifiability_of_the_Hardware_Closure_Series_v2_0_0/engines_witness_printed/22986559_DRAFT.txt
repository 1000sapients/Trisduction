3. 3. THREE AXES LOCK ONE POINT
Three vectors in three dimensions fix one point, the volume they span, exactly when they are independent, and
the test of independence is the determinant: nonzero if and only if no axis lies in the plane of the other two. The
author’s codex proves this lock over the two-element field by exhaustion, and the RH closure uses it as the shape

4. 4. THE COLOR SINGLET IS THE DETERMINANT
Quantum chromodynamics gives each quark one of three colors, and every free particle ever observed is a color
singlet: confinement, observed and not derived. For three quarks there is exactly one singlet, the totally antisym-

5. 5. THE LOCK FAILS WHEN TWO AXES COINCIDE
Put two of the three colors on the same axis and the determinant vanishes (lock_fails_when_axes_coincide;
computed in Appendix B): two planes meet in a line, and no point is fixed. In the proton this is antisymmetry at
work: three quarks cannot share a color, and the only color-neutral state of three is the one that vanishes when
any two coincide. The template’s coplanar collapse, the failure mode of its lock, is the reason there is no singlet
of three identical colors.

7. 7. THE WHOLE’S SPIN IS NOT READ FROM THE PARTS
The proton has spin one half. In 1988 the European Muon Collaboration found that the quarks’ spins carry only

differently and both total one half (quark_record_does_not_decide). The record of the parts does not decide the
whole: the two-worlds theorem, measured in a collider. The whole keeps its bit, the spin one half, and the parts’
record does not return how.
