:::intro
Sūrat Qāf is the fiftieth sūra of the Qurʾān, forty-five verses, Meccan, and it takes its name from the single disconnected letter standing at its head. It opens the Ḥizb al-Mufaṣṣal, the last of the seven manzils, and the record holds that the Prophet ﷺ recited it at the two ʿīds and from the pulpit often enough that one of the women of the Anṣār reported learning it from his mouth. That report is transmitted material and enters here as witness only.

The sūra is built on one axis and states it in its own rhyme. The word *baʿīd*, far, falls three times and only three times: the deniers open with it, saying the return is a far return; the yoked companion closes with it at the judgment, saying the man was in far error; and between the two the text negates the word outright, bringing the Garden near, *ghayra baʿīd*, not far. Against that stand *aqrab*, nearer than the vein of the neck, and the caller who calls from a near place. Distance is what the covered eye reports and nearness is what the text states.

The arc runs in one motion. An oath, an objection, and then the answer to the objection given not as argument but as a thing standing in front of the objector: a sky built without a seam, ground spread and moored, water sent down, dead land revived, and the emergence named as the same motion under a particle of sameness. Then the roll of those who denied before, then the interior, the whispering self and the nearness closer than the neck's own vessel, the two seated receivers and the ready watcher. Then the crossing: the stopping-up of death, the horn, the driver and the witness, and the cover lifted so the sight goes iron. Then the two ends, the cavity that asks whether there is more and the Garden brought forward to close range with the more held at the source. The sūra closes on patience, on the swimming praise before sunrise and before setting, on the caller from the near place, and on the one instruction the messenger is given about force: he is not set over them as one who compels.

This gloss runs at full density. Every content word carries its transliteration, its root, and its concrete floor, in that order, on first occurrence in the unit; particles run plain, and a word already glossed runs plain thereafter. The floor is excavated under the Concreteness Hierarchy, the body first, then the field and sky, then motion, and the abstract sense opened only after the physical one. Where a root genuinely carries two senses that are not synonyms, both print. Where a reading is contested, the contest prints with it. Honorifics stand outside the apparatus and are never glossed.

The blocks below carry their grade on the header. **Head at verse N** marks a division the text supplies, where the block's first verse states an operation and the verses under it supply its instances, checked against three marks. **Reader's cut** marks a division supplied here, for pace or because the passage's genre bars a head at intake. The distinction is printed because a reader who cannot tell an authored cut from a found one is reading the scribe's structure as the text's.
:::

:::closing
What the sūra established, it established by refusing to argue. The objection was that a return across dust is a far thing, and the reply set a seamless vault, a moored ground, water that comes down, and a tract brought back from death, and then joined the two with a particle of sameness rather than of likeness. The emergence is not illustrated by the cycle. It is named as the cycle, and the objector is standing in it while he objects.

The through-line binding the sections is the near and far axis carried in the rhyme. *Far* is spoken three times and every time by someone who cannot see: the deniers at the third verse, the yoked one at the twenty-seventh, and then the word itself is negated when the Garden is brought forward. What lies between those uses is the account of why a covered eye reports distance. The heedlessness at verse twenty-two is *ghaflah*, from the root of the unbranded beast and the tract without a landmark, and the knowing at verse four is from *ʿalam*, the landmark itself. One root gives the mark, the other its absence, and the sūra sets them eighteen verses apart on the same axis. The cover is then lifted with a possessive on it, *your* cover, and the sight goes iron. Nothing crossed a distance. A lid came off.

The core claim is stated twice in one word. The cavity asks *hal min mazīd*, is there more, and receives no answer; five verses later the Garden's people are told they have what they will, and the more is stated rather than asked and given an address, *ladaynā*, with Us. The one who asks is inside the thing that cannot be filled. The one who receives is not asking, and what exceeds his wish is held at the source. Between those two verses stands the only verb the Garden needs: *uzlifat*, it was brought forward, passive, not travelled to.

And the sūra ends by taking force off the table. The last instruction to the Messenger ﷺ is that he is not set over them as a *jabbār*, from the root that sets a broken bone and also compels. He is given the reminding and refused the compelling. A gate that must be entered in wholeness cannot be forced from outside, and the sūra closes on exactly that.

**Division of the blocks, reported.** Nine blocks, four found and five authored. The found heads stand at verses 6, 12, 16 and 31, each meeting the marks: the head states an operation and the body supplies its particulars, no new operation opens inside the block, and a root at the head returns at or near its foot. Three blocks are barred at intake, verses 1-5 on the formula opening the corpus itself controls at Q 38:1-2, verses 19-26 on narrative sequence, and verses 27-30 on dialogue. Two hold the marks only partially, verses 36-38 and 39-45, and partial does not meet the bar. The ratio is printed because a unit reporting all-found has fitted its cuts to the pattern, and the cheapest check on that is the count. And the division carries evidence about composition history in neither direction: a found head is not evidence of unitary composition and an authored cut is not evidence of a seam, since a hypothesis confirmed by the pattern holding and equally confirmed by its breaking cannot be refuted by either.
